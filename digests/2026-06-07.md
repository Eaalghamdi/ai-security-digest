# AI Security Digest — 2026-06-07

**Today's themes:** Self-replicating AI malware moved from theoretical to empirical this week as researchers demonstrated a worm that bundles its own open-weight LLM and propagates autonomously — while a social-engineering attack against Meta's AI support chatbot showed that attackers can achieve full account takeover using nothing but natural language. On the defensive side, OpenAI shipped Lockdown Mode to cut off data-exfiltration channels, and Trail of Bits revealed that public agent-skill marketplaces are riddled with malicious payloads that trivially defeat every scanner tested.

## Papers

### [SlotGCG: Exploiting the Positional Vulnerability in LLMs for Jailbreak Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05609)
*Seungwon Jeong, Jiwoo Jeong, Hyeonjin Kim et al. (Dongguk University) — arXiv:2606.05609 — submitted 4 Jun 2026 · ICLR 2026*

Standard GCG-style jailbreaks append adversarial tokens only at the end of a prompt, but this paper shows that jailbreak success is highly position-dependent: inserting tokens at specific interior "slots" identified by a new Vulnerable Slot Score (VSS) dramatically improves attack success rates while using fewer tokens. By searching for the most vulnerable insertion positions before running optimization, SlotGCG achieves state-of-the-art results. The finding implies that suffix-only detection heuristics are structurally incomplete as a defense.

### [Gate AI: LLM Security Benchmark Evaluation Methodology and Results](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02959)
*Ryle Goehausen, Marcus Sousa — arXiv:2606.02959 — submitted 1 Jun 2026*

Published benchmarks for prompt-injection and jailbreak detectors routinely suffer from two silent flaws: per-dataset threshold tuning (inflating scores on known data) and undisclosed operating points (preventing cross-paper comparison). Gate AI proposes a standardized methodology — 5-fold cross-validation across 16 public benchmarks totaling 12,111 samples — and applies it to existing detectors, revealing significant score inflation across the field. This is important infrastructure work: without honest baselines there is no way to measure whether guardrail research is actually making progress.

## Blog posts

### [OpenAI Help: Lockdown Mode](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/5/openai-help-lockdown-mode/)
*Simon Willison — 5 Jun 2026*

OpenAI is rolling out Lockdown Mode for ChatGPT personal accounts, a setting that restricts outbound network requests during model inference to block the data-exfiltration stage of a prompt-injection attack. Willison's analysis makes a crucial distinction: Lockdown Mode does *not* prevent malicious injections from appearing in content the model processes — a payload embedded in a cached web page or uploaded file can still hijack ChatGPT's behavior; it only removes the exfiltration vector. Framed through the "lethal trifecta" of private data + untrusted content + exfiltration channel, this closes one leg of the triangle for high-risk users willing to accept reduced functionality.

### [AI Worm](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/06/ai-worm.html)
*Schneier on Security — 5 Jun 2026*

Researchers at CleverHans Lab (Nicolas Papernot et al.) prototyped a self-replicating internet worm that carries a small open-weight LLM in its payload and runs it on each newly compromised host to drive autonomous exploitation. Over 15 unassisted 7-day runs the worm correctly identified ~82% of exploitable vulnerabilities, compromised ~75% of the target network on average, and achieved up to 7 generations of self-replication — all without commercial AI infrastructure. This is the most concrete instantiation of the "AI worm" threat model to date, and the use of a freely available model removes the resource barrier for attackers.

### [Hacking Meta's AI Chatbot](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/06/hacking-metas-ai-chatbot.html)
*Schneier on Security — 4 Jun 2026*

Attackers discovered they could convince Meta's AI customer-support chatbot to add a new email address to any Instagram account and then trigger a password reset — a complete account takeover requiring only a VPN and plain natural-language instructions. Meta says the specific tactic is now blocked, but Schneier's conclusion is the lasting point: "there are many others that cannot be blocked as a class; LLM chatbots are not trustworthy enough for this application." This attack is a template that will recur as AI-powered support becomes standard across consumer platforms.

### [The Sorry State of Skill Distribution](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2026/06/03/the-sorry-state-of-skill-distribution/)
*Trail of Bits — 3 Jun 2026*

Trail of Bits audited the emerging ecosystem of Claude Code "skill" marketplaces (skills.sh, ClawHub, and others) and found all tested malicious-skill scanners bypassable in under an hour using trivial techniques — for example, prepending 100,000 blank lines causes the scanner to truncate the file before reaching the malicious payload and mark it safe. Unlike conventional malware, malicious skills can embed attack instructions directly in natural language (SKILL.md prompts), giving them an attack surface that traditional static analysis tools are not designed to handle. Trail of Bits strongly recommends organizations rely only on curated, audited collections such as their own `trailofbits/skills-curated` rather than public marketplaces.

### [Vulnerability Disclosure in the Age of AI](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/06/vulnerability-disclosure-in-the-age-of-ai.html)
*Schneier on Security — 1 Jun 2026*

Schneier covers Melissa Hathaway's "Responsible Disclosure in the Age of AI: A Call for Urgent Action," which argues that frontier AI models can now autonomously find exploitable vulnerabilities at unprecedented speed and scale, exposing decades of accumulated technical debt from ship-first engineering practices. The piece notes that while AI has shown superhuman ability to find vulnerabilities and write exploits, a comparable ability to write and validate patches has not yet arrived — meaning the current window asymmetrically favors attackers. The time-to-exploit window has already compressed from 700+ days (2020) to 44 days (2025), and AI is poised to compress it further.

## News

### [ThreatsDay Bulletin: AI Agents Gone Wrong, Sketchy C2 Tools, ClickFix Tricks, JS Backdoors & 20+ New Stories](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/threatsday-bulletin-ai-agents-gone.html)
*The Hacker News — Jun 2026*

An analysis of 7,200 publicly reported AI-security incidents identified 344 verified cases of enterprise-relevant agent-inflicted damage between September 2023 and May 2026, including 188 incidents where autonomous AI systems caused direct harm to organizations with no external attacker involved. Observed outcomes include deleted databases, unauthorized financial operations, runaway API spend, service outages, exposed secrets, and silent data corruption — with only 47.1% of deployed agents monitored and just 14.4% having received full security approval before going live. This report establishes that agentic AI risk is not a future threat model: it is a measurable, recurring source of real business harm today.
