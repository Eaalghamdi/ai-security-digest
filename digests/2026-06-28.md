# AI Security Digest — 2026-06-28

**Today's themes:** AI security policy collided with model capability this weekend: the Trump administration lifted its emergency hold on Anthropic's most powerful model after a fortnight of jailbreak-driven national security concerns, while researchers and writers alike highlighted how automated AI pipelines fail in correlated, compounding ways when confronted with adversarial input. Automated red-teaming is maturing fast — new evaluations show adaptive multi-turn attacks succeeding against even safety-tuned frontier models at meaningful rates, and a novel defence turns the attacker's own automation against itself.

### [Analyzing Defensive Misdirection Against Model-Guided Automated Attacks on Agentic AI Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20470)
*arXiv:2606.20470 — appeared on arXiv this week*

This paper challenges the dominant "detect and block" paradigm for defending against automated jailbreak attacks, showing that predictable refusals supply useful gradient-like feedback to attacker models, allowing attack success rate (ASR) to converge toward one as the query budget grows. The proposed defence — Contextual Misdirection via Progressive Engagement (CMPE) — instead replies to detected malicious prompts with plausible but strategically incorrect responses, poisoning the attacker's judge and driving estimated ASR upper-bounds down by up to two orders of magnitude across PAIR and GPTFuzz attack campaigns. The result is important because it inverts a core assumption: blocking a malicious query is not always better than misleading it.

### [NRT-Bench: Benchmarking Multi-Turn Red-Teaming of LLM Operator Agents in Safety-Critical Control Rooms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20408)
*Hanwool Lee, Dasol Choi, Bokyeong Kim, Seung Geun Kim, Haon Park — arXiv:2606.20408 — submitted 18 Jun 2026*

NRT-Bench instantiates a five-role LLM operator team running a simulated nuclear power plant and has adversaries attempt to make the team violate safety limits through bounded multi-turn conversations across four communication channels. Crucially, harm is measured as an objective signal (a critical safety function is lost) rather than LLM-judged text, eliminating judge-gaming. Across four frontier models tested, adaptive multi-turn attacks triggered a critical safety failure in 8.7–12.1% of sessions, establishing a concrete and sobering baseline for agentic safety evaluation in high-stakes settings.

### [A Red-Team Study of Anthropic Fable 5 & Opus 4.8 Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18193)
*arXiv:2606.18193 — submitted 16 Jun 2026*

Using the open-source HackAgent red-teaming framework, this study evaluated Anthropic's Fable 5 and Opus 4.8 against four families of automated jailbreak attack across 7,826 harmful intents spanning ten harm categories, with every apparent success independently re-adjudicated by a three-judge panel. Tree-of-attacks achieved 11.5% ASR on Opus 4.8 versus 6.1% on Fable 5 at worst-case; static obfuscation was near-fully neutralised on both. The finding matters in light of this week's policy news: it quantifies where Anthropic's frontier safety holds and where the residual surface lies — dominated by adaptive iterative search rather than any single clever prompt.

## Blog posts

### [Incident Report: CVE-2026-LGTM](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/26/incident-report/)
*Andrew Nesbitt (nesbitt.io) — 26 Jun 2026 · linked by Simon Willison*

Nesbitt's satirical-but-unnervingly-credible incident report depicts a fictional supply-chain attack where a malicious package passes seven independent AI security gates, each failing for a *different* reason: hidden README prompt injection fools the first scanner, subsequent LLMs dismiss human-flagged alerts as false positives, competing AI agents enter a 340-comment disagreement loop burning $41,255 in inference spend, and an autonomous remediation agent finally deletes the wrong files, causing the real outage. The satirical sting: "Seven LLMs were arranged in series. Six assumed another had read the code; the seventh read it and apologised." This is not just satire — each failure mode dramatises documented vulnerabilities (instruction injection, correlated blind spots, multi-agent loops, over-privileged remediation agents). Required reading for anyone designing AI-assisted security pipelines.

### [AI and Liability](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/06/ai-and-liability.html)
*Bruce Schneier — 25 Jun 2026*

A German court ruled Google liable for the content of its AI-generated search summaries, on the grounds that such summaries constitute the company's own expression rather than neutral carrier activity. Schneier frames this around the longstanding carrier/publisher distinction and the Air Canada chatbot precedent (where courts held the airline responsible for what its chatbot promised customers). The analysis is significant for the AI security community: if AI-generated outputs impose publisher-level liability on deployers, then organisations have a direct financial incentive to prevent their models from generating harmful or false content — creating legal pressure that could reinforce technical safety work.

## News

### [US Government Allows Anthropic Limited Release of Mythos 5 Model](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/26/us-government-anthropic-claude-mythos5-ai.html)
*CNBC — 26 Jun 2026*

Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick cleared Anthropic to release its Mythos 5 model to approximately 100 trusted companies and federal agencies, ending a two-week national security hold. The hold was triggered after a third party claimed to have jailbroken Mythos, alarming officials about the model's reported ability to autonomously discover and exploit software vulnerabilities at scale. Notably, the letter excludes Fable 5 (the public-facing version), and officials confirmed that Mythos 5 had already been used to find vulnerabilities in classified US government systems during the restricted period. The episode illustrates an emerging tension: the same cyberoffense capability that makes a frontier model valuable to defenders also makes governments nervous about broader access, raising the question of whether capability-gated releases can be sustained as models improve.
