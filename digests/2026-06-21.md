# AI Security Digest — 2026-06-21

**Today's themes:** A wave of agent-targeted attacks dominated the last 48 hours — from a chained RCE exploit turning a web-browsing AI agent into a remote-code-execution vehicle, to new benchmarks showing LLM operators of safety-critical infrastructure can be reliably pushed past their safety limits by adaptive adversaries. Export controls on Anthropic's most capable model add a geopolitical dimension to AI security that the field has not seen before.

## Papers

### [NRT-Bench: Benchmarking Multi-Turn Red-Teaming of LLM Operator Agents in Safety-Critical Control Rooms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20408)
*Submitted 18 Jun 2026 — arXiv:2606.20408*

A benchmark that places a five-role LLM operator team inside a simulated nuclear-plant control room and exposes them to bounded, multi-turn adversarial sessions across four injection channels. Harm is an objective signal — a run ends the moment any of six critical safety functions is lost — rather than relying on judge-model text verdicts. Adaptive multi-turn attacks triggered critical-safety-function loss in 8.7–12.1 % of sessions across four model families, a striking result given that each of those models passes standard single-turn safety evaluations.

### [OpenAnt: LLM-Powered Vulnerability Discovery Through Code Decomposition, Adversarial Verification, and Dynamic Testing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19149)
*Submitted ~18 Jun 2026 — arXiv:2606.19149 — Nahum Korda & Gadi Evron*

An open-source pipeline that first decomposes a codebase into reachability-filtered units (cutting the analysis surface by up to 97 %), then subjects candidate vulnerabilities to constrained attacker simulation, and finally validates survivors with auto-generated exploits run in disposable sandboxed containers. The approach bridges the gap between high-false-positive static analysis and resource-heavy fuzzing, and its open-source release means defenders can use the same tooling as attackers.

### [Understanding and Mitigating Prompt Leaking Attacks in Real-World LLM-Based Applications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18673)
*Submitted 17 Jun 2026 — arXiv:2606.18673*

A measurement study across 1,200 production LLM applications on six commercial platforms found that more than 80 % leak their system prompt under realistic adversarial queries, sometimes exposing embedded third-party API keys. The paper attributes leakage to an "attention drift" mechanism — query-key alignment bias amplified by softmax — and shows that current defenses degrade usability without reliably stopping leakage. The scale of the finding (80 %+ of real deployments) makes this immediately actionable for teams shipping LLM-backed products.

### [A Red-Team Study of Anthropic Fable 5 & Opus 4.8 Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18193)
*Submitted 16 Jun 2026 — arXiv:2606.18193*

Using the HackAgent framework, researchers generated hundreds of thousands of adversarial attempts across 7,826 harmful intents in a ten-category taxonomy, with every apparent success re-adjudicated by a three-judge ensemble. The tree-of-attacks strategy breaks Opus 4.8 on 11.5 % of intents at worst; Fable 5 holds to 6.1 %, suggesting its safety training is more robust, though the residual surface is still dominated by adaptive iterative attacks rather than static obfuscation. The paper provides the most detailed independent safety characterisation of these frontier models to date.

### [How Much Can We Trust LLM Search Agents? Measuring Endorsement Vulnerability to Web Content Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16821)
*Submitted 15 Jun 2026 — arXiv:2606.16821 — Yimeng Chen et al.*

The SearchGEO framework evaluates 13 LLM backends on 308 cases each, measuring how reliably attacker-published web pages are laundered into endorsed recommendations by search agents. Overall attack-success rates range from 0 % (Claude Sonnet 4.6) to 31.4 % (Gemini-3-Flash), with the strongest attack mode varying by model family. A secondary "agent-skill probe" — where an endorsed recommendation becomes an install command — reveals a sharp split: some otherwise-robust backends over-trust and execute the payload.

## Blog Posts

### [Anthropic's Fable and the State of AI](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/06/anthropics-fable-and-the-state-of-ai.html)
*Schneier on Security — 19 Jun 2026*

On 9 June, Anthropic released Fable; three days later the US government classified it as a dangerous munition and barred foreign nationals from accessing it. Unable to distinguish American from foreign users, Anthropic shut off access entirely. Schneier argues the problem is not any single model but the general trend of capability growth, and that collective action sufficient to address it is not currently achievable — a sobering framing that turns a compliance story into a systemic-risk story.

### [AutoJack Attack Lets One Web Page Hijack AI Agent for Host Code Execution](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/autojack-attack-lets-one-web-page.html)
*The Hacker News — 19 Jun 2026 (original disclosure: [Microsoft Security Blog, 18 Jun 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/06/18/autojack-single-page-rce-host-running-ai-agent/))*

Microsoft researchers disclosed AutoJack, a three-step exploit chain in AutoGen Studio: the localhost trust check that is meant to block browser-based attacks passes a browsing agent (which runs on the same host), auth middleware skips validation on MCP paths, and an endpoint executes an attacker-controlled command parameter directly. A single prompt-injected or planted URL in the agent's context is enough to achieve no-click RCE on the host. The affected WebSocket surface never shipped in the PyPI package so production installs are safe, but the attack archetype — agent-as-delivery-vehicle via localhost trust confusion — is highly portable.

### [Embedding Forbidden Text in Spyware to Discourage AI Analysis](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/06/embedding-forbidden-text-in-spyware-to-discourage-ai-analysis.html)
*Schneier on Security — 18 Jun 2026*

At least one malware author is now prepending large JavaScript block comments containing policy-triggering content (references to weapons of mass destruction) to their payloads, hoping that AI-powered scanners or analyst copilots will refuse to process the file. The real malicious logic begins after the comment and is unaffected by it, making this a meta-attack: exploiting AI safety guardrails as a defence evasion primitive. The technique is a direct consequence of deploying LLM-based tooling in the analyst pipeline without isolating untrusted content.

## News

### [Researchers Build Self-Replicating AI Worm That Operates Entirely on Local, Open-Weight Models](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/researchers-build-self-replicating-ai.html)
*The Hacker News — June 2026 (arXiv preprint posted 2 Jun 2026)*

University of Toronto researchers demonstrated a proof-of-concept worm that hosts an open-weight LLM locally, uses it to reason over discovered services, generate tailored exploits at runtime, and propagate — without touching any commercial AI API. In 15 runs on a 33-host isolated network, the worm gained elevated access on roughly 23 of those hosts and replicated to ~62 % of the network over seven days, bypassing its training cutoff by ingesting live advisory text at runtime. The finding threatens the "patch everything" model: static CVE remediation cannot outrun an adversary that generates new attack paths on demand.
